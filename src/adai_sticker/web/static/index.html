adAI Sticker Lab

Make a sticker that fools an AI

Image-recognition AI can name a thousand kinds of objects in a photo, but it doesn't see the way you do. In about five minutes you'll design a sticker that makes an AI see something that isn't there, and find out why that's possible.

Why AI vision can be tricked

Models learn shortcuts: patterns of texture and color that usually go with a label. A sticker can be packed with those patterns.

What makes an attack strong

You'll test your sticker at every size, angle and lighting, and on AIs it was never designed for.

Where the AI is looking

Heat maps show the AI staring at your sticker instead of the real object in the photo.

Before you start

Adversarial stickers are real security research, and they can cause real harm. By continuing you agree to:

  • Use stickers for learning, research, and fun with your own things.
  • Never use them to fool safety systems: cars, cameras, alarms or content filters.
  • Never use them to harass, mislabel or embarrass people.

Which AI do you want to fool?

Each of these models learned to recognize objects from over a million labeled photos. They were built in different ways and in different years. Bigger isn't always harder to fool.

What should the AI see?

Pick something that clearly isn't in your photos. That's what makes the trick convincing. The sticker will try to make the AI say this label no matter what else is in the picture.

    Fooling rate on practice photos

    What's happening? The computer pastes your sticker into batches of practice photos at random sizes, angles, positions and lighting, then checks what the AI sees. After each batch it nudges every pixel a little in whichever direction makes the AI more likely to say your target.

    Researchers call this Expectation over Transformation: the sticker gets optimized for the average photo, not one perfect photo, so it keeps working in the real world.
